Why Stratagram

A diagram is a conversation, not artwork.

General-purpose drawing tools give you a thousand shapes and a blank page, and every diagram ends up looking different. Stratagram gives you a small, fixed visual language for networks, and it does the layout. You spend the time on the idea.

⚡

Ideas first

Type Firewall, Router, switch|core-01 and press Enter. Drag from a border to connect. Pick a layout and the diagram arranges itself. A sketch for a meeting takes minutes, not an afternoon.

◆

One visual language

Every device type has its shape, symbol and color. Every line type says what it carries: a trunk is a double line, a tunnel a band, routed links show their interfaces. There are no styles to invent.

⇄

Made to be shared

Because everybody draws with the same stencils, a diagram from a colleague reads at a glance. Frames carry title, site, revision and a legend. Files are plain JSON, exports are SVG, PNG and draw.io.

Standardized stencils

28 device types, one look.

Routers, switches, firewalls, load balancers, wireless, servers and clients, virtual and distributed virtual switches, the internet, and cloud providers with their own tiles. The type sets outline, symbol and color, the same in every diagram, on screen and in every export.

  • Grouped by category in the type list; quick add knows short forms like fw, rtr, sw, lb, dvs.
  • Ten line types: 802.1Q trunk, L2 switched, L3 routed, L3 subinterface, IPsec, VXLAN, CAPWAP, SD-WAN, MPLS, plain.
  • Legends in the frame list the line types a diagram uses.
Fast

From empty page to topology in one line.

Quick add, connect by dragging, auto layout. The editor fills in what a whiteboard sketch leaves out: ports where lines meet, straight lines, room for labels.

  • Auto layout: hierarchy (top down or left to right, from the internet down to the clients), radial, circle, grid, force, or just tidy.
  • Lines that do not cross: auto layout lets a line leave its socket the other way, picks another route, or swaps neighbors, so as few lines cross as the ports allow; right-angled lines side by side each keep a lane of their own.
  • Smart guides: hold Ctrl while dragging and items line up, ports snap level so lines run straight.
  • Drop a device on a line to put it in between; bend lines through points; lines to nowhere for "out there".
  • Edit in place: double-click and type; undo for everything.

Down to the port

Switches and patch panels, front side up.

When it matters which socket a cable is in, switches, routers, firewalls, servers and patch panels show their front: numbered ports, odd on top and even below, RJ45, SFP, LC duplex or SC. Each socket is a connection point.

  • Any port count; the panel grows with it, SFP uplinks in a group of their own.
  • Cables plug in: a line ends in the middle of its socket (between the halves of a duplex LC).
  • Front and back: a rear port comes out right under its socket, so the way through the panel can be followed.
  • Cable the back by drag: draw a line to the panel's edge under a socket and, before you let go, the socket lights up and its tie shows, so you see which front port the cable ends up behind.
  • Opt-in: a switch, router, firewall or server stays a simple box until you give it ports.

Dragging to the back of socket 7: it lights up before the drop

Layers

Show what the audience needs, keep the rest.

Layers and sub-layers, as deep as you like, shown, hidden or locked together. Drag a layer to reorder it or onto another to put it underneath; the new parent lights up.

  • Cabling stays end to end: hide the patch panels and each device's line goes straight to the switch port it is patched to.
  • Backgrounds group a layer's items; layer names can show on their devices.
  • Sheets hold several pages in one file.

Deep zoom

Zoom into a device, keep drawing.

Turn on Zoom into (Ctrl+I) and the view zooms in without limit. Zoom into a server until it fills the screen and draw its VMs, its vSwitch and their lines inside it, at normal size. Zoom back out and the diagram looks exactly as before: the overview stays clean, and the details are there for whoever goes looking.

  • Any depth: each level is 8x smaller than the one around it, a rack in a site, a server in the rack, the VMs in the server.
  • Fades in and out: the inside shows from 400 % on, fully from 600 %; the grid and snapping follow the level.
  • Stays with its device: move the server and everything inside goes along; lines may run from inside out.
  • In every export, each item at its own scale.

Ready to hand over

Frames, legends, exports.

23 frame templates, from a plain outline to ISO title blocks, revision tables, change and incident sheets, each with title, author, site, classification and the date of the last change. Set your company's defaults once.

  • SVG and PNG for documents, wikis, tickets and slides.
  • draw.io, editable, with ports and connections; paste back and forth with draw.io too.
  • Cisco Modeling Labs: turn the drawing into a lab topology to run.
Themes

Light, dark, neon.

Pick a theme in the toolbar and the whole editor follows: the canvas, the panels and the drawing itself. Fills turn deep, lines and outlines bright, text light, so a diagram reads as well at night as on paper.

  • Your colors carry over: a light blue switch turns deep blue, custom colors keep their hue, cloud providers keep their brand colors.
  • The file stays the same: a theme changes how it is drawn, not what is saved, so everyone opens it in their own.
  • Exports follow: SVG and PNG come out in the theme you work in, also from the command line with --theme; draw.io keeps the file's colors.
  • Neon adds a soft glow to lines and outlines, on screen and in SVG.
Follow the cable

Which port, which cable?

On a switch with 48 sockets, finding where a line ends takes a moment. Stratagram shows it: select a line and its ports light up, point at it and it tells you what it connects.

  • Select a line: the ports at both of its ends light up and show their names.
  • Point at a line: a note names its two ends (device and port, or socket), the run end to end when it goes on through patch panels, and its type and label.
  • Through hidden layers too: with the patch panels hidden, the line straight from switch to server can still be selected and tells what is behind it.

Browser

Get started

Build it

Stratagram builds from source with Rust 1.85 or newer and Qt 6.8 or newer.

Install Qt 6

For example on Debian or Ubuntu; Fedora and Arch have the same as qt6-qtdeclarative-devel qt6-qtsvg-devel and qt6-declarative qt6-svg.

$ sudo apt install qt6-base-dev qt6-declarative-dev qt6-svg-dev qml6-module-qtquick \
    qml6-module-qtquick-controls qml6-module-qtquick-layouts qml6-module-qtquick-dialogs

Build and run

$ git clone https://github.com/shinsterneck/stratagram.git && cd stratagram
$ cargo build --release
$ ./target/release/stratagram examples/demo.stratagram

Or in the browser

$ rustup target add wasm32-unknown-unknown
$ web/build.sh && python3 -m http.server -d web/www 8000
NetBox plugin · NetBox 4.5 to 4.7

Your NetBox, drawn.

The plugin adds a Diagram tab to every site and location. It draws the site from what NetBox knows (devices, cables, circuits, patch panels) with Stratagram's stencils, and keeps your arrangement when NetBox changes.

From NetBox

Drawn from your source of truth.

No more redrawing a site by hand after every change. The site comes in with its devices and cables, laid out from the internet down to the servers.

  • Device types from roles: firewall, router, core and access switches, servers, with your own mapping where roles say something else.
  • Line types from interfaces: tagged ports make trunks, routed ends make L3 links, access ports L2.
  • Right-angled lines by default, each in a lane of its own and laid out with as few crossings as the ports allow (or straight, as you set it up).
  • Circuits become internet clouds named after provider and circuit; patch panels get one socket per front port, rear ports behind the sockets they map to; switches their physical ports, copper first and SFP as uplinks, each cable in its port.
  • Locations get sheets of their own; a location's Diagram tab opens its sheet.
Stays yours

Refresh, don't redraw.

Arrange the diagram, add notes, a frame, layers, and save it in NetBox. When NetBox changes, Refresh from NetBox brings it up to date without moving anything you placed.

  • New devices and cables are added beside what they connect to; renamed ones are renamed.
  • What is gone is set aside on a layer "Gone from NetBox", not silently deleted.
  • Layers by type and per device: show or hide all patch panels, or one server; cabling stays end to end along NetBox's cable paths, and a line through hidden panels can still be selected and tells what it connects.
  • One undo step; saved diagrams are in NetBox's change log.
Themes

Light or dark, like your NetBox.

The editor starts in NetBox's own light or dark mode and switches along with it. Pick Dark or Neon in its toolbar to keep one; the browser remembers it. The saved diagram keeps its colors, so everyone sees it in their own theme; SVG and PNG exports come out in the one in view.

Access

Everyone sees what they may see.

The data comes from NetBox's own models, filtered by each user's object permissions, with no API tokens. Viewing, adding and changing site diagrams are NetBox permissions of their own, constraints included.

Install

Add it to NetBox

Build the package

Needs Rust with the WebAssembly target, wasm-bindgen-cli 0.2.129, cargo-about, and Python's build module.

$ netbox-plugin/build.sh      # → netbox-plugin/dist/netbox_stratagram-*.whl

Install it into NetBox's environment

$ sudo /opt/netbox/venv/bin/pip install netbox_stratagram-1.0.0-py3-none-any.whl

Enable it in configuration.py

PLUGINS = ["netbox_stratagram"]
PLUGINS_CONFIG = {
    "netbox_stratagram": {
        "kinds": {"dmz-host": "server"},   # device type by role, before the built-in guesses
        "all_devices": False,             # also devices without cables
        "type_layers": True,              # a layer per device type and per device
        "routing": "orthogonal",         # or "straight", "around"
        "fronts": ["switch"],            # drawn with their ports; also "router", "firewall", "server"
    },
}

Migrate, collect, restart

$ cd /opt/netbox/netbox
$ sudo ../venv/bin/python manage.py migrate
$ sudo ../venv/bin/python manage.py collectstatic --no-input
$ sudo systemctl restart netbox netbox-rq

Then grant the object type NetBox Stratagram › Site Diagram (view, and add and change to save) to the users who should see the Diagram tab.
